## Business

Nike designs, markets and distributes athletic footwear, apparel and equipment through wholesale and NIKE Direct, with Converse a separate brand. FY2026 revenue was $46.398 billion, versus $46.309 billion in FY2025 and $51.362 billion in FY2024; diluted EPS fell from $3.73 in FY2024 to $2.16 and $2.10. NIKE Brand contributed $45.222 billion and Converse $1.174 billion. FY2026 NIKE Brand geographic revenue was $20.511 billion North America, $12.572 billion EMEA, $5.847 billion Greater China and $6.243 billion Asia Pacific/Latin America. Selling branded product is not the same economic model as a pure recurring royalty franchise. [issuer-annual]

Almost all manufacturing is outsourced. Vietnam, Indonesia and China produced approximately 52%, 27% and 16% of Nike footwear, respectively, with four suppliers accounting for 60%; the top three US wholesale customers accounted for 29% of US sales. Brand demand is geographically diverse, but production concentration exposes tariffs, labor standards, logistics and geopolitical disruption. Wholesale sell-in can improve reported revenue while retail sell-through remains weak; returning to wholesale is useful distribution repair, not proof of restored full-price demand. [issuer-annual]

**October 1 FY2027 Q1 disclosure is substantially worse than a historical EPS screen suggests.** Revenue was $11.213 billion, down 4% reported/5% currency neutral, net income $712 million and diluted EPS $0.48. NIKE Direct fell 8%, digital 13%, owned stores 5%; wholesale declined 1%, Greater China revenue 22% reported and Converse 28%. North American wholesale growth partly offsets weakness elsewhere. Gross margin rose 60bp to 42.8%, primarily lower warehousing/logistics costs, but that is not an all-clear on demand. [issuer-q1]

FY2027 issuer guidance is high-single-digit revenue decline and **adjusted** diluted EPS $1.15–$1.35, excluding approximately $0.15 Pace restructuring. It is a forecast, not reported GAAP EPS. Pace targets $2.5 billion cumulative savings through FY2031 before approximately $1 billion pre-tax charges and reinvestment; approximately $0.3 billion charges are expected in FY2027. Cumulative savings cannot be treated as annual recurring profit, and the program adds execution and organizational risk during brand repair. [issuer-q1]

Inventory ended FY2026 at $7.501 billion versus $7.489 billion, with receivables rising to $5.931 billion from $4.717 billion. Q1 FY2027 inventory was $7.846 billion, down 3% year-on-year but above May’s balance because dates/seasonality differ. OCF fell from $7.429 billion in FY2024 to $3.698 billion and $2.868 billion, with cash capex $684 million in FY2026. Inventory-dollar improvement alone does not prove assortment freshness or a full-price channel reset; monitor markdowns, returns, mix and wholesale receivables. [issuer-annual; issuer-q1]

## Moat

Nike’s brand, athlete associations, innovation and distribution are real intangible assets; $4.754 billion FY2026 demand-creation expense illustrates both investment capacity and the ongoing cost of maintaining relevance. Performance-led product franchises can win technical credibility, while Jordan and sportswear add cultural demand. The consumer can switch easily, fashion cycles move quickly, and competing specialist brands can take running or lifestyle share without recreating Nike’s full scale. [issuer-annual; analysis]

The moat test is sustained full-price sell-through, product innovation and healthy wholesale partner economics rather than restoring the prior DTC mix at any cost. The Win Now approach includes reducing classic-footwear supply, clearing excess assortment and restoring retailer relationships; October commentary still identifies NIKE Sportswear, Jordan and Greater China as needing substantial work. A turnaround requires refreshed demand, not merely lower headcount or a higher marketing budget. [issuer-annual; issuer-q1]

## Management

Elliott Hill and CFO Dave Denton describe performance-business progress but explicitly acknowledge unresolved sportswear, Jordan and China issues. Pace adds supply-chain modernization, an India enterprise campus, three-geography alignment and organizational reductions. That operational redesign can improve productivity; it can also disrupt relationships and product execution. Treat the savings target as a gross management objective subject to local-law implementation, charges and reinvestment. [issuer-q1]

The 2022 $18 billion repurchase authorization had acquired 124.4 million shares at average $97.57 for $12.1 billion through May 2026; reapproval in June did not create a new $18 billion authorization. FY2026 buybacks were only $146 million versus $2.985 billion a year earlier, while dividends were $2.407 billion. Price decline alone does not prove prior purchases irrational, but it illustrates how buybacks fail to protect owners when earning capacity erodes. Dividends and product/brand reinvestment compete with weaker cash generation. SBC of $715 million remains a recurring cost. [issuer-annual]

No independent executive incentive or insider ownership analysis is claimed. Alignment should be judged by sell-through, channel repair, inventory/cash quality and returns after brand reinvestment, not by earnings adjusted to remove repeated restructuring costs. [analysis]

## Inversion

1. **Secular brand/share loss rather than temporary inventory clearance:** performance momentum fails to spread, competitors own key categories and fashion relevance deteriorates. Current sportswear/Jordan/China admissions make this a live risk. [issuer-q1]
2. **Wholesale repair masks demand:** sell-in raises revenue/receivables while partners require discounts, returns and promotional support; DTC shrinkage reduces economics faster than wholesale expansion can replace them.
3. **Turnaround costs repeat:** restructuring, marketing and reinvestment absorb gross Pace savings; cumulative savings through FY2031 are confused with annual profit.
4. **Tariff accounting obscures margins:** FY2026 included a $986 million tariff-recovery benefit in cost of sales, largely offsetting tariffs incurred; $302 million was received and $684 million remained receivable. Removing the recovery without addressing the associated tariffs would create a misleading normalization. FY2026 also included $385 million severance, while the new release refers to approximately $0.3 billion prior severance under the program. Those disclosures have different scopes/rounding and are not silently forced into a fabricated exact bridge. [issuer-annual; issuer-q1]
5. **Supply chain/China/capital returns:** manufacturing concentration, local demand/FX, trade policy and shareholder distributions consume room for recovery. The low historical P/E is a denominator trap when new guidance is far lower.

The bear case models continued earnings erosion and a lower multiple; permanent loss can exceed it. The 11.5% required return prices remaining fashion/turnaround uncertainty, not all failure in one discount-rate premium. Reject the constructive recovery thesis if full-price demand, wholesale sell-through, receivable cash collection and inventory health fail to improve together, or repeated restructuring becomes necessary simply to support dividends.

## Industry

Athletic demand contains durable sports participation and less durable lifestyle/fashion cycles. Consumers can choose other global brands, focused running innovators, local Chinese labels or discounted inventory. Shelf space, athlete endorsements and technical credibility matter, but large marketing spend cannot force category leadership. Outsourced production reduces owned-factory capex while leaving inventory and trade exposure on Nike’s balance sheet. [issuer-annual; analysis]

Current-quarter China and Converse contractions, alongside weaker Direct, demonstrate asynchronous regional/category conditions. Consumer spending, tariffs and currency affect margins; they should not be turned into unsourced sector forecasts. Treasury’s 5.24% 10-year reference sets a high opportunity cost for waiting several years for an uncertain recovery. [issuer-q1; treasury-20261009]

## Decision

**Pass for now at $34.71; turnaround watchlist, not a “cheap at 16.5×” recommendation.** Historical P/E uses FY2026 $2.10, but the forward outlook is sharply lower. Starting from the deliberately depressed $1.10 earning-capacity assumption yields only 4.21% base price CAGR and $24.46/share weighted discounted modeled value against an 11.5% hurdle. The bull case can reward successful brand restoration, yet the downside is not bounded by historical cash or yesterday’s brand multiple.

Revisit after several periods of product-led full-price growth, stabilizing Greater China/Converse, improving Direct without wholesale channel stuffing, working-capital cash release and credible **net** Pace savings. A lower price alone does not establish that the recovery earnings base is durable. Dividends are excluded from native return calculations and can be financed only by cash left after real reinvestment.
