## Business

### Long-duration Treasury exposure, not cash
Investors pay 0.15% annually for a tradable portfolio tracking the ICE U.S. Treasury 20+Year Bond Index. The index contains publicly issued, fixed-rate USD Treasury obligations with remaining maturity greater than 20 years and at least$300 million face value outstanding excluding Fed holdings. It is market-value weighted and updates on each month's last calendar day. Inflation-linked securities, Treasury bills, agency debt and stripped/zero-coupon issues are excluded [prospectus].

BlackRock Fund Advisors uses representative sampling; at least 80% of assets are index components and at least 90% are Treasury securities expected to support tracking; no more than 10% may be designated futures/options/swaps exposure under the described policy [prospectus]. October 9 has 47 holdings; October 8 displayed exposure is 100.00% Treasuries with cash/derivatives rounding 0.00% [sponsor]. Many issues do not diversify the sovereign or interest-rate factor.

Returns come from coupon accrual, price changes, reinvestment, rolling membership and costs. Monthly distributions are cash transfers out of NAV, not free additional return [sponsor]. There is no terminal maturity/redemption at par for the ETF unit. [INFERENCE] Owning TLT through time is different from holding one fixed Treasury until maturity: bonds leave the eligible bucket and the fund maintains long exposure.

## Industry

TLT sits in the long end of nominal US sovereign fixed income. Alternatives include Treasury bills, intermediate/shorter-duration funds, TIPS, direct fixed maturities or stripped bonds. Their duration, inflation sensitivity, roll economics and cash-flow timing differ materially; none is automatically interchangeable with TLT's 20+year coupon-bond mandate [prospectus].

[INFERENCE] Bond issuance, investor demand, inflation expectations, real yields and term premium all affect long-end pricing. A central-bank policy change may influence those variables without determining the long end. Government debt is an asset here, not the ETF's corporate debt. No corporate TAM, operating margin or revenue trend applies. A nominal Treasury claim protects neither purchasing power nor short-horizon market principal. The sponsor June 30 factsheet effective duration 15.31 years versusOctober 8 duration 14.68 years reflects changing yield/portfolio conditions; it is not a constant risk coefficient [factsheet, sponsor].

## Decision
