## Business

Uber is a local liquidity marketplace across Mobility, Delivery and Freight, monetizing transactions, memberships and advertising. FY2025 gross bookings were $193.454 billion and recognized revenue $52.017 billion; Mobility revenue was $29.670 billion, Delivery $17.248 billion and Freight $5.099 billion. Gross bookings are customer transaction value, not company revenue, and customer spending is not all distributable to shareholders. Drivers, couriers, merchants, insurance, incentives, payment fees and local compliance are necessary counterparties to the platform. [issuer-annual]

**Reported revenue/bookings is not a uniform economic take rate.** Freight uses different gross revenue recognition; Mobility and Delivery include differing principal/agent arrangements, incentives and advertising. In Q2 2026, the UK business-model change moved $1.1 billion of driver payment cost from cost of revenue to contra-revenue, reducing headline revenue growth about eight percentage points without an underlying economic impact. Mobility revenue margin therefore fell mechanically; treating that as competitive take-rate collapse would be wrong. Conversely, comparing an aggregate margin after the change with earlier periods without adjustment overstates comparability. [issuer-q2]

Q2 gross bookings reached $58 billion, up 22%, with trips up 18% and users up 16%; revenue was approximately $14.2 billion, up 11% on the altered basis. Mobility segment operating income was 7.6% of bookings and Delivery 3.8%, each a segment measure rather than a consolidated GAAP net margin. Advertising exceeded $2.5 billion annualized revenue run-rate and Uber One represented more than 70% of Delivery bookings. Those monetization streams improve potential margin mix, but memberships require subsidies/benefits and advertisements need merchant returns. [issuer-q2]

Cash generation is genuinely substantial: FY2025 OCF $10.099 billion and cash capex $336 million. Quality is less simple than “asset-light means all cash is owner earnings”: OCF adds back $1.826 billion recurring stock compensation and benefitted from a $2.2 billion working-capital inflow, primarily insurance reserves and accrued liabilities exceeding claims payments. Restricted cash/investments largely collateralize insurance; those balances are excluded from freely deployable cash. Native historical FCF is before repurchases, acquisitions, finance-lease principal and other investment commitments. [issuer-annual]

The October 6 agreed ezCater acquisition is an **announced, not closed**, $2.3 billion all-cash transaction. ezCater reported over $2.5 billion trailing gross bookings, high-teens growth, over 140,000 restaurants and average orders over $400; non-GAAP operating profitability and expected margin accretion are not acquired GAAP earnings today. Q2 remarks also disclose approximately $4 billion invested in a roughly 37% economic Delivery Hero stake and a proposed remaining-share acquisition targeted for second-half 2027. Funding those initiatives while pursuing AV investments means old year-end cash is not a current acquisition budget. [acquisition; issuer-q2]

## Moat

Dense supply/demand reduces wait times and unpaid driver miles, which can improve reliability without proportionally raising incentives. A global app, payments infrastructure, safety operations and cross-product distribution are advantages over a small city entrant. Uber One reached 46 million members at December 2025; users active in both Mobility and Delivery generated more than three times bookings of single-product consumers, and 58% of first-time Delivery users were new to the platform. Those correlations support cross-selling opportunity, not proof that membership caused incremental spending or that it is all high-margin. [issuer-annual]

Switching costs remain limited: drivers can multi-home, riders compare prices, restaurants use several channels, and competitors are geographically concentrated. Local marketplace scale does not imply an irreversible worldwide network effect. Advertising may deepen merchant dependence, but extracting too much can push orders to competing/direct channels. A moat test is retained engagement and earnings per transaction **after** incentives, insurance, worker benefits and marketing rather than gross-bookings growth alone. [issuer-annual; analysis]

## Management

FY2025 repurchases were $6.523 billion, with $27 billion combined authorizations and $19.2 billion remaining at year-end. Q2 2026 buybacks of $510 million coexist with major strategic investments, so neither authorization nor headline FCF guarantees ongoing share shrinkage. Assess net actual shares against stock compensation and acquisition financing, not diluted EPS averages alone. The retained actual cover-page count is 2,058,115,983 on February 10, not year-end diluted weighted-average shares. [issuer-annual; issuer-q2]

Management’s AV strategy now entails over $10 billion of future equity, infrastructure and offtake commitments, with partners committed to roughly 120,000 vehicles over coming years. External financing is being explored, not guaranteed. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi’s acquisition rationale emphasizes corporate catering distribution, but proving integration return and capital discipline matters more than calling every adjacency a synergy. Executive compensation and personal insider ownership have not been independently corroborated here. [issuer-q2; acquisition; analysis]

## Inversion

1. **Autonomy removes the aggregator:** Waymo, Zoox or Tesla controls scarce AV supply and direct demand, or captures most economics from Uber while retaining its own consumer channel. Partnerships can expand Uber distribution without creating proprietary autonomy. Conversely, unsuccessful fleet partners can strand commitments. [issuer-annual; issuer-q2]
2. **Regulation reprices supply:** worker classification, minimum compensation, collective bargaining, insurance and local licensing raise structural costs; price increases reduce demand. Existing Proposition 22 protections do not settle all countries, past periods or challenges. Mexico and European rules create different exposures. [issuer-annual]
3. **Cash conversion reverses:** insurance claims consume prior reserve-driven working-capital cash, indirect-tax assessments require cash before appeal, and FCF exceeds sustainable distributable funds. SBC dilution is a recurring economic expense.
4. **Capital-intensive adjacencies:** Delivery Hero, ezCater and AV commitments compete with buybacks; forecast earnings growth fails to pay for integration or an increased capital base. Q2 unrestricted cash plus short-term investments was only $5.4 billion, distinct from restricted balances and investment marks. [issuer-q2; acquisition]
5. **GAAP valuation trap:** FY2025 $10.053 billion attributable net income and $4.73 diluted EPS include a $5.0 billion tax valuation-allowance release; FY2024 had a $6.4 billion tax benefit. Q2 2026 net income includes a $1.6 billion pre-tax investment-revaluation gain. These are not recurring platform operating growth. [issuer-annual; issuer-release; issuer-q2]

The bear case represents economics stagnating while the multiple falls, not zero revenue growth or an absolute downside floor. The 11.5% hurdle prices residual model/funding uncertainty above Treasury; it does not replace explicit classification/autonomy failure cases. Evidence against the thesis would be persistent incentive dependence, falling underlying segment profitability after accounting reconciliation, uncontrolled insurance settlements or AV capital at inadequate returns.

## Industry

Mobility competes with private cars, taxis and public transport as well as Lyft, Bolt, Didi and Ola; Delivery with DoorDash, Instacart and direct restaurant/retailer fulfillment. Freight has established brokers and cyclical shipment demand. The addressable pool is large, but substitutable demand and regional competitors restrain platform rent extraction. Regulation can simultaneously consolidate market share and reduce margins. [issuer-annual]

Autonomy is both a route to lower ride cost and an industry power transfer. Q2’s seven live AV cities and a target of up to fifteen by year-end are early deployment scale, not proof of favorable mature unit economics. Treat lower labor cost as a potential shared surplus, not automatically Uber’s profit. Treasury 10-year 5.24% also makes speculative cash-flow deferral more expensive. [issuer-q2; treasury-20261009; analysis]

## Decision

**Watchlist; no new buy at $71.51 under this hurdle.** Base price-only CAGR is 8.66%, below the 11.5% required return, and weighted discounted modeled value is $62.27/share. The observed GAAP P/E near 15× is artificially comforting because tax benefits elevate its denominator. Operating economics are improving, but autonomy and acquisitive capital allocation widen the outcome distribution.

Revisit on comparable UK-adjusted economics, claim-payment/insurance-reserve conversion, actual net share shrinkage, ezCater closing/integration terms and mature AV contribution **after fleet capital**. Dividends are excluded from native returns, and no precise probability can be verified from source documents. This is a research ranking rather than trade advice.
