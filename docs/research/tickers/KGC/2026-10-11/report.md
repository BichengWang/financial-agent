## Business

Kinross sells gold rather than a differentiated consumer product. USD metal sales were $7.0511 billion in 2025, versus $5.1488 billion in 2024 and $4.2397 billion in 2023; common-shareholder IFRS earnings were $2.3901 billion versus $948.8 million and $416.3 million. Realized gold price was $3,423/oz versus $2,393/oz, while attributable all-in sustaining cost (AISC) was $1,571/oz versus $1,388/oz. The earnings expansion therefore cannot be treated as evidence of independent volume or brand pricing power. Gold price and mine cost spread dominate the result. [issuer-annual]

The operating portfolio comprises US mines including Fort Knox/Manh Choh, Round Mountain and Bald Mountain, Brazil’s Paracatu, Mauritania’s Tasiast and Chile’s La Coipa; Canada’s Great Bear and Chile’s Lobo-Marte are development options. Management expected 66% of 2026 production outside the US. Tasiast and Paracatu account for more than half of production, so diversification by mine name does not eliminate concentrated geological or jurisdictional exposure. Manh Choh is 70% owned, but reported cash statements consolidate 100%; native consolidated FCF is not the issuer’s attributable FCF. [issuer-annual]

**The September 23 update supersedes the earlier approximately 2-million-ounce production narrative.** Attributable production is now expected at 1.84–1.86 million gold-equivalent ounces in both 2026 and 2027, 2%–3% below the prior guidance low end. La Coipa suffered extreme winter weather and higher copper/lower recoveries in sulphide feed; stockpiling that material for potential future treatment is not production today. Round Mountain Phase S has lower mining rates, grades and recoveries, with both delayed ore and outright lost output. AISC guidance rises to $1,850–$1,900/oz and production cost of sales to $1,420–$1,460/oz. This is a real operational impairment of the near-term thesis even though Paracatu/Tasiast remain strong. [issuer-update]

Gold reserves are wasting assets. Year-end 2025 proven and probable reserves were 20.9 million ounces, down from 21.9 million after roughly 2.1 million ounces depletion and 1.2 million ounces additions. Measured/indicated resources of 27.5 million ounces are separate, not additional proved cash flows; conversion requires drilling, metallurgy, economics and permits. Reserve life cannot be estimated responsibly by dividing group reserves by current production alone because mine sequencing and recoveries differ. Great Bear first gold is targeted for 2029, Lobo-Marte for the early 2030s; these are development forecasts, not existing recurring earnings. [issuer-annual]

FY2026 attributable capex was guided at $1.5 billion, comprising approximately $450 million sustaining and $1.05 billion non-sustaining, with similar overall spending anticipated in 2027–2028. Low net borrowings help finance that pipeline, but the equity still bears overrun, tailings, closure and reclamation obligations outside a simple debt-minus-cash comparison. Great Bear/Phase X/Curlew spending is necessary reinvestment, not optional cash automatically distributable to owners. [issuer-annual]

## Moat

The defensible asset is access to permitted ore bodies, processing infrastructure and experienced operators. Scale can spread engineering and procurement costs; established low-cost assets can survive a gold downturn better than marginal entrants. Neither assures the sale price. Replacement reserves become more valuable only if they can be mined at acceptable incremental returns. [issuer-annual; analysis]

The falsification test is reserve additions and sustaining economics **after** capex, taxes and closure liabilities, not a higher accounting margin in a gold rally. The September recovery issues demonstrate why ore quality, plant fit and mining execution matter more than a generic “gold scarcity” moat. Lower mine recoveries can destroy equity value even when the gold commodity remains attractive. [issuer-update; analysis]

## Inversion

1. **Gold mean reversion:** revenue falls faster than mining/fixed costs; a P/E built from $1.95 historical EPS looks deceptively cheap. Reclamation and growth capex do not fall automatically with gold.
2. **Geological/processing failure:** La Coipa high-copper feed proves structurally lower recovery; Round Mountain bridge ore underdelivers before Phase X. September guidance is already evidence, not a hypothetical straw man. [issuer-update]
3. **Jurisdictional/physical loss:** Mauritanian security/tax risk, Brazilian currency and tailings risk, Chilean water/permitting/weather and Canadian project approvals impair cash flow. More than half of production concentrated in two mines magnifies a shutdown. [issuer-annual]
4. **Development capital trap:** $1.5 billion annual attributable capex and concurrent development absorb a commodity windfall; new production arrives later or at lower returns. Resource ounces fail to convert to economic reserves.
5. **Accounting optimism:** $116.1 million impairment reversal, $53 million Asante gain and a $56.1 million reclamation recovery appear among adjustments; $231.7 million Chirano disposal consideration is not recurring operating cash. The adjusted $1.84 is a starting assumption, not a through-cycle guarantee. [issuer-annual]

Reject the long thesis if recovery/grade issues persist across reporting periods, projects lose their cost/timing discipline, reserve depletion outruns economic additions or shareholder returns require debt during weaker gold markets. These operating risks are encoded in bear/base cases; the 12% hurdle compensates residual commodity/jurisdiction and forecast uncertainty, not a substitute for modeling them.

## Industry

Gold is influenced by real rates, dollar movements, reserve diversification and risk aversion; Kinross is a leveraged mining exposure, not bullion. Higher nominal gold can coincide with higher diesel, wages, royalties and taxes. The September cost guidance explicitly assumes gold $4,350/oz and oil $100/barrel for the remainder of 2026; neither is a sourced October 11 spot-price observation. Approximately 70%–80% of costs are USD-denominated, and local-currency moves still matter. [issuer-update]

Competition is for land, permits, skilled labor and project capital more than for gold customers. With Treasury’s October 9 10-year par yield at 5.24%, mine risk needs substantially more compensation than a government obligation. Reserve/resource optionality may deserve value, but no reserve NAV or project DCF has been claimed here. [treasury-20261009; analysis]

## Decision

**Pass at $23.74 for a fundamentals-led compounder allocation; commodity watchlist, not a claim that gold will fall.** Base-case price-only CAGR is negative and weighted discounted modeled value is materially below the quote. Historical FCF yield is attractive but cyclic, with materially greater near-term capex and a recent production reset. The underwriting requirement is a larger price cushion or evidence that operating recoveries and replacement reserves sustain cash after development spending.

Revisit after La Coipa sulphide-recovery evidence, Phase S/Phase X sequencing, revised multi-year production/capex and Great Bear permit/cost milestones. The model omits dividends and does not price every reserve/project separately; it must not be used as a literal mine NAV or a portfolio purchase authorization.
