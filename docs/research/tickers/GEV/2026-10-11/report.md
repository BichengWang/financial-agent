## Business

Utilities, independent power producers, industries and governments pay GE Vernova for equipment that generates/transmits reliable electricity and for service that sustains asset output; switching an installed turbine’s critical parts and servicing is harder than choosing the next new-build supplier [issuer-10k]. The alternative depends on the segment: Siemens/Mitsubishi gas turbines, rival grid equipment or competing wind technology—not one interchangeable “AI power” product.

Segment totals include intersegment eliminations; margins exclude depreciation/amortization and are not corporate GAAP operating margin [issuer-fy25]. That cost structure is direct evidence to distinguish service value from equipment headline growth, without inventing a cash-margin ratio [issuer-fy25].

The 2025 filing reports roughly 7,000 installed gas turbines, 1,800 under long-term service agreements with average remaining life roughly ten years; service know-how and uptime matter. Wind has approximately 59,000 onshore turbines but about 24,000 service agreements: an installed base is not all contracted recurring revenue [issuer-10k]. No exact single-customer revenue concentration percentage is retained. Utilities provide diversification, but utility/sovereign counterparty, geographic, supplier and AI-capex concentration risks are not thereby eliminated.

## Management

CEO Scott Strazik’s focus on underwriting and installed-base profitability has observed backing in Power/Electrification margins, but Wind must be judged on delivered losses, not consolidated uplift [issuer-fy25, issuer-q226]. FY2025 buybacks consumed $3.316B, 8.2M shares at average $406; H1 2026 buybacks were 4.3M shares at $854 average, with YTD total capital returned $3.9B. The price paid rose materially: prudent repurchases require a normalized intrinsic-value case and enough cash for advances-funded contracts, not just a rising order book [issuer-fy25, issuer-q226].

The remaining Prolec stake cost $5.275B, concentrating capital in grid demand and adding capacity; reported H1 remeasurement profit is not cash synergies. Planned manufacturing/R&D expansion, pension contributions and separation obligations compete with dividends and buybacks. Compensation/proxy ownership and independently assessed succession depth were not retained. ## Industry

[INFERENCE] Electrification and reliable dispatchable power persist beyond a particular AI architecture. Power generation, grid connection, cooling and site execution are complementary bottlenecks, not additive ownership of the same total market. GEV supplies the first two and services an established fleet; hyperscaler demand is not the only source of utility investment. Gas equipment faces fuel economics/emissions permits, wind faces grid/policy and installation issues, and nuclear commercialization has its own development timelines.

Issuer expects gas output capacity 20GW in Q3 2026, 24GW in 2028 and 30GW in 2030; these are capacity plans, not observed delivered output. Electrification data-center orders exceeded $5B year-to-date, but FY2026 guidance still includes approximately $400M Wind losses [issuer-q226]. No external numerical TAM or capacity-share forecast is invented. The industrial tailwind fails to accrue if advance payments stop, fixed-price costs outrun agreed prices or competitor supply removes premium economics.

## Decision

**Watchlist, provisional.** Business quality is strongest in installed Power services, promising in grid equipment, and weak/unsettled in Wind underwriting. Durability confidence is moderate-high for service know-how, moderate for equipment pricing and low for consolidated cash extrapolation. Stewardship evidence is mixed-positive operationally but buyback/acquisition prices need a normalized valuation. Price attractiveness is not established by this package.

Review triggers: separately profitable installed-base service, improving Wind contract estimates, sustained reservation-to-order conversion, credible recurring-tax earnings bridge, Prolec integration and cash conversion after advance-funded backlog delivery. Thesis breaks if equipment backlog margins fail at execution or if Wind obligations consume Power/grid surplus. A five-year trading closure would be easier to tolerate with the service fleet than with a scarcity-multiple narrative. Research confidence in observed disclosures is distinct from investment certainty.
