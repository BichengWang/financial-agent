## Business

Server/cloud operators, PC OEMs, gamers and embedded designers pay AMD for performant CPUs, GPUs and adaptive platforms because workloads and energy budgets demand continuing upgrades; alternatives are NVIDIA, Intel, Arm-based custom CPUs and custom accelerators. EPYC CPU economics and Instinct GPU scaling are related but not identical franchises.

[INFERENCE] Historical technological leadership is not a valuation floor. No probability is assigned to catastrophic Taiwan or counterparty default events.

## Industry

Foundry capacity, advanced packaging and HBM sit upstream of CPUs/accelerators/network systems; software compatibility and customer operating economics determine downstream adoption. AMD challenges both x86 competitors and NVIDIA, while Arm/custom-chip platforms attack the same compute budgets [issuer-quarter], [issuer-latest]. [INFERENCE] Inference expansion can broaden workloads enough for multiple suppliers, but hyperscalers' custom architectures and portable frameworks can capture profits that investors expect merchant chips to earn. Product-specific TAM and independent dollar market-share estimates were not recovered; issuer platform superlatives and GW intentions are not converted into guaranteed sales. EPYC/client/embedded provide cycle diversification, not immunity from aggregate capital-spending retrenchment.

## Decision

**Watchlist business, price diligence required.** Duan: customers pay for real compute utility; Buffett: CPU compatibility is sturdier than a temporary accelerator lead; stewardship: engineering execution merits attention, while equity concessions and guarantees demand scrutiny; Munger: rising sales can coexist with poor incremental per-share economics; Li Lu: compute productivity is durable but shareholder capture is not automatic.

Review when clean continuing earnings, actual cash collections and per-share growth validate deployment announcements; track net share issuance/warrant milestones, ROCm production reliability, inventory reserves and guarantee exposure. Thesis break: sustained subsidies/discounts are needed for AI adoption while per-share earnings cannot outgrow customer concessions. AI research confidence medium on disclosures and low-to-medium on economic normalization; investment certainty remains low given current price and architecture competition.
