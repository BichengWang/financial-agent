## Business

Customers or financiers pay Bloom for rapidly deployable onsite solid-oxide fuel-cell power, installation/consulting and maintenance because power availability can be more valuable than simply the cheapest electricity; alternatives include the grid, gas engines/turbines, batteries plus grid supply and other fuel cells [issuer-fy25, issuer-q226, sa-quote]. This is equipment plus lifetime obligations, not a software subscription or a merchant utility.

### Backlog, working capital and funding
Approximate $20B total backlog is not $20B certain near-term cash: product backlog around $6B incorporates anticipated tax incentives, while service commitments span 5–20 years and permit annual convenience termination [issuer-fy25]. Product and service backlog cannot be capitalized on the same margin or duration. Financier commitments, tax qualification, site approvals and customer credit determine conversion.

FY2025 OCF was $113.949M despite Q4 OCF $418.073M; inventory, contract assets and shrinking deposits consumed cash across the year. $2.5B debt proceeds explain much of the cash-balance growth, not retained operating profit. Related-party revenue was $892M in FY2025; Brookfield equity-method joint ventures incurred $40.421M losses, and Bloom invested $36.491M cash. Vendor excitement about hyperscalers therefore must be separated from credit exposure and the economics of financing intermediaries [issuer-fy25]. This is demonstrated transaction concentration, not a measured single-end-customer percentage.

Q2 2026 OCF $226.432M benefited from $211.693M deposit/deferred-revenue inflow while receivables and contract assets consumed cash. June cash was $2.666859B; common outstanding was 293,354,001, distinct from Q2 diluted weighted-average 323.331M and the October vendor count. Upfront share-based consideration to a customer’s customer produced $90.967M current and $215.533M long-term consideration assets; warranty accruals also rose. These commitments need collection/amortization and useful-life diligence, not an automatic “cash conversion solved” verdict [issuer-q226].

Sources: [issuer-fy25, issuer-q226]; causal consequences [INFERENCE]. The informed bear case is not that fuel cells fail to generate electricity: the power can be useful while financing subsidies, stack replacement and customer bargaining absorb the economic value. Avoid anchoring to record revenue or analyst price targets. Discrete warranty, legal and counterparty losses are not hidden inside a higher hurdle.

## Industry

[INFERENCE] Reliable electricity is an enduring productivity need; an AI buildout is only one buyer cohort. Bloom sits at generation beside gas engines/turbines and behind-the-meter energy arrangements, upstream of cooling and data-center construction. Onsite power may solve connection timing but still requires fuel, permits, redundancy and construction. Gas turbine shortages create an opening, not proof of permanent fuel-cell market share. Batteries principally shift energy and require a charging source; they are not assumed a complete baseload replacement.

Issuer forecasts FY2026 $3.9–4.2B sales and $2.55–2.85 **non-GAAP** EPS following Q2, versus much lower initial guidance, but forecasts are neither received cash nor GAAP common earnings [issuer-q226]. No numerical TAM, carbon-cost estimate or market-share trajectory is invented. Tailwind fails to accrue if customer financing economics worsen, sites stall, or superior substitutes remove the time-to-power premium.

## Decision

**Watchlist, provisional; business opportunity credible, price attractiveness unestablished.** Duan’s understandable-customer-need test is met, but lifecycle and funding economics remain outside a high-confidence circle of competence. Buffett-style durability is not established by a temporary connection bottleneck; Munger inversion puts collections and stack liabilities before sales; Li Lu’s power-productivity tailwind does not tell us who retains surplus.

Review triggers: sustained positive GAAP diluted EPS across cycles, service-adjusted cash generation without deposit acceleration, timely related-party collection, normalized warranty cost, funded site conversion and dilution discipline. Thesis breaks if customers’ delivered-cost advantages depend on disappearing credits or financing support, or if service cash costs erase hardware margins. AI research confidence is moderate for statements and low for customer economics; neither implies investment certainty or an allocation recommendation.
