## Business

Arm licenses processor architecture/IP and earns royalties as partners ship Arm-based chips. License fees may recognize episodically; royalty flows depend on customer shipments, mix, contractual rates and collections. OEMs, chip designers and cloud operators pay because energy-efficient compatible compute and developer ecosystems are valuable; alternatives include x86, RISC-V and existing architecture licensees designing their own cores. Architecture ubiquity is not equivalent to Arm earning a fixed percentage of every system's value.

## Industry

Arm sits at IP/architecture layers above EDA/foundry production and below operating systems/application workloads; silicon entry moves it downstream into direct supply/qualification risk. [INFERENCE] AI agents increase general-purpose orchestration and efficient CPU demand alongside accelerators, but NVIDIA/AMD stacks and hyperscaler custom CPUs can capture system value. Data-center use can diversify handset sensitivity without erasing cyclicality. RISC-V offers an open architecture alternative; technical and software migration costs determine its actual threat [issuer-fy]. Numerical serviceable TAM is omitted because promoted compute penetration does not define Arm's monetizable revenue or a verified merchant share. Energy efficiency is societally useful but not a proof of current stock-price attractiveness.

## Decision

**Watchlist, no validated price target.** Duan: a real understandable licensing need; Buffett: architecture switching costs attractive, rate capture/partner neutrality less certain; stewardship: concentrated controller and SBC need minority-owner discipline; Munger: successful compute adoption can fail shareholders through valuation/dilution; Li Lu: energy-efficient compute has long-term relevance without automatically rewarding ADS owners at this quote.

Review triggers: recurring royalty progress versus one-off licenses, measurable diluted EPS after compensation, production gross margin/capex/cash collections, partner response to AGI CPU, Arm China settlement patterns and SoftBank pledged ownership changes. Thesis break: architecture importance persists but royalties stagnate while Arm-funded production destroys incremental cash economics. Missing clean normalized EPS is a genuine limitation, not an invitation to manufacture a target. Confidence medium-high on annual mechanics/ADS basis and low on new-production margins; investment certainty low.
