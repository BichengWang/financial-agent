## Business

### The fund service and underlying economics
Investors pay the trust's annual 0.0945% expense ratio for tradable S&P 500 exposure instead of maintaining hundreds of individual holdings. The benchmark is float-adjusted market-cap weighted across eleven GICS sectors; the sponsor seeks price and yield correspondence before expenses. The fund is a unit investment trust, not an operating business that earns a fee on its own AUM for its shareholders [sponsor]. Fee revenue accrues to service providers; shareholders receive portfolio returns less costs.

Underlying companies sell goods/services, retain earnings, invest and distribute dividends. Those economic drivers, valuation changes and changes in index membership determine long-run fund results. Index replacement helps keep exposure representative but does not eliminate buying expensive incumbents. [INFERENCE] Broad participation in productive US enterprises is an intelligible long-term proposition; equating it to a guaranteed annual return is not.

The sponsor reports 504 fund holdings versus 503 index holdings on October 8; the vendor displays 505 without the same explicit date [sponsor, sa-overview]. These counts include different portfolio/ancillary conventions; they are not distinct operating segments. A five-year corporate revenue/cash-flow series is inapplicable. Sponsor September 30 annualized NAV returns are 15.60% one-year, 13.65% five-year and 15.18% ten-year, with reinvested distributions and net fees; these are historical total returns, not forecasts [sponsor].
