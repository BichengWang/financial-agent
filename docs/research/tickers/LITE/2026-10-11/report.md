## Business

Economic quality depends on the specific bill of materials, qualification and capacity, not simply “AI exposure.”

## Inversion

The strongest bear case is a capacity-rich optical cycle meeting concentrated hyperscaler demand just as architectures change. Revenue can remain above old levels while margins, working capital and per-share earnings disappoint.

## Industry

[INFERENCE] Moving more data with less energy is enduring demand; whether a particular module, laser or circuit switch captures lasting rents depends on architecture and manufacturability. Materials/laser suppliers feed optical modules, switch/accelerator platforms define sockets, contract manufacturers assemble, and hyperscalers buy infrastructure. Copper remains an alternative at short reach; silicon photonics/CPO/NPO can change component content rather than simply eliminating all optical suppliers. The retained peer analysis identifies Coherent as a scaled InP/laser competitor adding substantial capacity; competitive technology and supply responses are real, not a single predetermined route [sec-api-analysis].

## Decision

**Watchlist for operating quality; no substantiated margin of safety.** The right-business case is credible optical demand and qualified manufacturing. Durable pricing power is not established through a complete supply cycle; stewardship has financed growth but changed the per-share denominator. Munger-style inversion prioritizes concentrated customers and fixed capacity; Li Lu's productivity framework supports optical bandwidth demand without dictating winners. Research confidence is moderate for operating results, lower for recurring EPS/capital structure; economic certainty is lower than the apparent publication volume.

Review triggers: new primary cash-flow/concentration disclosures; inventory versus shipped revenue; net capacity additions/ASP; conversion and preferred-share reconciliation; independently observed qualification/customer retention; shipment realization of OCS/CPO design wins. Thesis breaks if capacity-driven cash absorption and dilution persist despite demand growth. A five-year market closure would require customer durability and solvent funding, not a favorable analyst target. No orders or allocation recommendation follow from this analysis.
