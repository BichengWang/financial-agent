## Business

Device makers, enterprises and consumers pay for NAND-based storage because they need durable data at acceptable speed, power and cost; alternatives include rival flash products and, for appropriate workloads, HDD or other storage tiers. Sandisk designs chips/controllers/firmware and integrates storage systems, with wafer supply through Flash Ventures with Kioxia. The filing says all flash memory is obtained from these ventures, in which Sandisk owns 49.9%; control is joint, not a wholly owned fab [issuer-filing].

## Moat

| Candidate | Evidence | Counterevidence / falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| NAND technology and scale | Shared process development and eight Japan manufacturing facilities through Kioxia ventures [issuer-filing] | Kioxia is also a competitor; Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and YMTC can expand supply |
| Enterprise qualifications | Datacenter revenue grew to $5.153 billion [issuer-annual] | Growth combines price and volume; no independently observed retention or share series establishes lock-in |
| Controllers/system engineering | In-house designs and vertically integrated solutions | Foundry/wafer dependence, technology obsolescence and OEM bargaining remain |

Demand and capacity discipline can generate temporary scarcity rents without a durable moat. The key falsifier is pricing reverting despite higher bit demand, or process/yield disadvantage forcing higher cost. Ten-year excess returns require technology execution and disciplined joint-capacity economics, not just the current pricing curve.

## Inversion

| Failure path | Timing / severity | Observable warning / treatment |
|---|---|---|
| NAND supply catches demand | Within a technology/capacity cycle; severe margin compression | Falling prices despite bit shipments; bear EPS -25% CAGR and 10× multiple |
| Prepayment economics reverse | Contract timing; liquidity/refund risk | Refund liabilities, renegotiations, deposits versus recognized revenue; native FCF cannot value obligations |
| Kioxia relationship or Japan production disrupted | Discrete severe supply tail | Joint-venture governance, outages, technology delays; no fake probability |
| Enterprise migration stalls | Multi-quarter qualification cycle | Datacenter mix/volume, returns and customer acceptance; bull requires genuine durable adoption |

The informed bear case accepts rising storage demand but argues that this extraordinary margin and pricing environment invites competitive capacity. The legacy memory cycle is an analogy, not proof that the new contracting model fails. No scenario represents manufacturing catastrophe, partner disputes, a future tax-separation liability or total commodity collapse.

## Industry

Sandisk is a flash producer/system integrator; Western Digital's HDD business is outside this security. AI datasets, inference and edge storage create bit demand, while compression, workload architecture, retention choices and storage tiers limit capture. Flash Ventures spreads capital/technology cost but does not erase reinvestment or make Sandisk software-like. Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron and Kioxia are named competitors [issuer-filing].

The new long-term/prepayment model may improve demand visibility; contractual scarcity rents are not automatically ten-year price power. Enduring data growth should be distinguished from NAND dollar pricing. No unobserved industry TAM or price curve is supplied.

## Decision

Watchlist, with price unattractive under the base and probability-weighted discounted assumptions. Independent listing identity is established and the profitable FY2026 is real reported evidence, but normalized rent is not established. Review NAND ASP/bit trends, NBM refund/deposit terms, Flash Ventures capital commitments, enterprise qualifications and buyback price discipline. Thesis breaks if high reported FCF depends on refundable prepayments while pricing and margins normalize.

Duan's business/people lens separates customer usefulness from stewardship; Buffett's durability lens asks whether the franchise and cash survive a market closure; Munger's inversion emphasizes specific failure mechanisms; Li Lu's structural lens asks who captures technology gains. These are analytical lenses, not simulated endorsements. Confidence is moderate in observed financial extraction and low-to-moderate in conditional future value. Abundant consensus coverage cannot overcome unknown customer, legal or supply-chain terms.
