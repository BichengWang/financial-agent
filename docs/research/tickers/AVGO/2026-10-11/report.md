## Business

Broadcom sells scarce connectivity/custom-compute building blocks and enterprise infrastructure software. Cloud customers buy performance, energy efficiency, engineering execution and time-to-deployment; software customers pay to operate installed virtualized/private-cloud and enterprise workloads rather than undertake migration disruption [issuer-fy] [issuer-optics].

| Economic engine | Reported FY2025 revenue | What customers pay for | Constraint |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Semiconductor solutions | $36.858B | Custom accelerators, merchant networking, broadband/wireless/storage silicon | Hyperscaler program concentration, foundry/packaging supply, internal ASIC competition |
| Infrastructure software | $27.029B | VMware/enterprise operations, virtualization and infrastructure lifecycle | Renewal behavior, migration substitutes and licensing/customer friction |

FY2021–2025 revenue $27.450B/$33.203B/$35.819B/$51.574B/$63.887B and net income $6.437B/$11.223B/$14.082B/$5.895B/$23.126B show that reported growth and profit are not a smooth organic series [sa-financials]. Software revenue increased from $7.637B FY2023 to $21.478B FY2024 and $27.029B FY2025: VMware consolidation materially changes comparability, so this must not be cited as an organic SaaS growth rate [issuer-fy] [sa-financials].
Q3 FY2026 revenue $29.591B comprises $20.839B semiconductors and $8.752B software. Management says AI semiconductor revenue $16.7B, up 221%, a product classification within semiconductors, not an independently audited additional segment [issuer-q3]. Q4 $34.8B total and $21.7B AI revenue are guidance, not achieved revenue. Circle of competence covers design cycles, installed-software switching costs and consolidated cash generation; it does not extend to undisclosed XPU contract economics or customer capex financing.

## Moat

| Candidate | Evidence | Why it may decay | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom ASIC engineering/qualification | Broad silicon and interconnect portfolio; collaborative design and physical implementation capability | Hyperscalers hire internal teams, negotiate dual sourcing or choose rival Marvell | Program ramps occur but Broadcom's content/margins erode |
| Merchant networking integration | Switch/SerDes/DSP/optics portfolio and established deployment ecosystem | Customer standards and competitive silicon prevent permanent monopoly | Qualified competing switch silicon wins sustained high-volume production |
| Infrastructure-software switching cost | Large software revenue base after VMware completion | Price/licensing friction can fund migration to alternatives | Renewal cohorts/churn weaken despite near-term reported margin gains |
| Capital-allocation operating discipline | Large annual OCF and low cash capex | Acquisitions, leverage and aggressive exclusions can obscure true economic cost | Cash per common share stalls despite adjusted EBITDA growth |

[INFERENCE] Silicon rents derive from engineering scarcity and execution, not simply being “custom.” The customer owns the workload and can internalize architecture; customer-specific NRE/qualification may entrench a supplier within a generation yet strengthen buyer bargaining at the next node. Five end customers accounted for approximately 40% FY2025 revenue, per retained issuer filing search excerpt; the full filing fetch was blocked, so this is explicitly less complete than the release-based financial grounding [issuer-concentration]. Ten-year durability is **medium-high but bifurcated**: installed software persists, while each silicon cycle must re-earn design selection. No unsupported quantitative share/churn moat is assumed.

The announced $8.5B incremental pro forma EBITDA within three years was an acquisition target, not a measured realized VMware profit or stand-alone free cash flow [issuer-vmware-terms]. Avoid attributing all software earnings to VMware or comparing that target with consolidated EBITDA as proof of purchase success.
FY2025 acquired-intangible amortization **$8.062B**, SBC **$7.568B**, and cash-interest-associated expense **$3.210B** have different meanings. Amortization is a GAAP allocation of prior acquisition value and is mostly noncash now, but acquired software IP requires support/reinvestment and customer retention. SBC is a real ownership-transfer cost; interest is a financing burden. FY2025 GAAP tax benefit $397M includes a disclosed approximately $2.1B discrete noncash benefit related to statutes of limitation, so GAAP EPS is not a pure steady tax-rate run rate [issuer-fy]. Q3 amortization $2.006B, SBC $2.019B and interest $778M show these remain consequential [issuer-q3]. Neither blanket amortization add-back nor blanket equivalence of adjusted and owner earnings is defensible.

## Inversion

| Failure | Severity / clock | Warning | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| One major XPU customer internalizes implementation or dual-sources | High; multi-year design-cycle reset | Broadcom content falls as customer AI spend grows | Bear growth 6% and 22x multiple; catastrophic single-program loss unmodeled |
| Capacity/power bottleneck delays custom ramps | High near term | Forecast ramps exceed delivery/cash collection | Guidance excluded from reported facts |
| Software renewal friction destroys installed-base rents | Structural over several renewals | Migration activity/contract contraction despite short-term pricing gains | Base assumes persistence, not indefinite price extraction |
| Debt/capital commitments crowd equity cash return | High at stress/recession | Borrowings or contingent customer financing rise faster than durable cash | No claim that “noncash amortization” makes debt irrelevant |
| Optical integration cannibalizes discrete DSP economics | Cycle-dependent | CPO/LPO adoption shifts profit to another supplier layer | Requires portfolio adaptation; no generic optics TAM multiplier |

The strongest bear is not an AI spending collapse: it is robust customer spend with weaker supplier value capture, coupled with acquired software cash flows eroding after monetization. Public October news suggests potentially large customer-funding commitments; no verified primary contractual terms were retained, so these are diligence questions, not asserted off-balance-sheet obligations or inserted debt numbers. Do not quantify unverified financing as fact.

## Industry

[INFERENCE] Broadcom occupies both merchant silicon sold to system vendors such as Arista and customer-specific accelerator/interconnect engineering sold to hyperscalers. This gives portfolio breadth but creates customer/channel bargaining complexity. Marvell is a custom/connectivity alternative; Nvidia combines GPU, networking and system software; in-house silicon is both a demand source and a rival design option. Coherence, packaging, switch bandwidth, memory access and installed software support can matter more than nominal chip peak throughput.
Broadcom's March 2025 optical disclosure spans 6.4-Tbps XPU optical attach, Sian3 3nm 200G/lane DSP, 800G/1.6T optics, PCIe6 over optics, linear optics and 7m+ AEC solutions [issuer-optics]. These are announcements/demos, not independently verified full-volume adoption or field efficiency. [INFERENCE] Short copper can minimize optical component cost/power at usable reach; optics enables longer/high-density links. LPO/CPO can displace module DSPs or move value toward packaging/ASIC integration. Broadcom can participate on either side, but transition costs and cannibalization do not vanish because its portfolio is broad.
Software industry economics are distinct: subscription conversion/consolidation changes revenue recognition and renewal leverage, while migration cost supports rents; customers retain alternatives and budget ceilings. Acquisition-driven growth and merchant/custom product cycles should never be collapsed into a single “AI compounder” multiple without separate diligence. No independent TAM or realized VMware customer ROI is claimed.

The base terminal price approximately equals today's reference quote, while its discounted value is $202.05/share. At this entry, strong business growth can coexist with poor shareholder returns. ## Decision

**Watchlist, no validated margin of safety on the retained historical-capacity sensitivity.** Buffett's switching-cost lens applies differently to VMware and chip qualification; Duan's people/business lens requires verifying contract and capital-allocation economics; Munger inversion tests who owns scarcity and who absorbs funding risk; Li Lu's structural data-center opportunity does not imply monopoly equity returns at any price.
Priorities: primary current share count and preferred/commitment details; current end-customer concentration, NRE and ramp cash economics; software renewal/cohort attrition; acquisition-specific reinvestment and owner earnings; debt maturity/interest burden and any substantiated customer financing guarantees. Break the thesis if design-program content systematically falls, software churn outruns monetization, or leverage/commitments capture the equity cash cushion. An exchange closure would leave cash-producing installed products, but does not justify ignoring entry valuation or acquisition leverage.
