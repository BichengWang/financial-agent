## Business

McDonald’s is predominantly a franchisor and real-estate/lease intermediary, not a consolidated operator of every restaurant wearing its brand. It had 45,356 restaurants at December 2025, approximately 95% franchised. Systemwide sales of $139.4 billion include franchisee sales; recognized corporate revenue was $26.885 billion. Conventional franchisees generally fund equipment, signs, seating and decor, while McDonald’s owns or leases land/buildings and receives sales-based royalties and rent with minimums. Typical 20-year contracts preserve site control at expiration. Developmental licensees and affiliates fund more local capital; China/Japan equity-method earnings differ from consolidated restaurant revenues. [issuer-annual]

FY2025 franchise revenue of $16.548 billion comprised $10.442 billion rent, $6.018 billion royalties and $88 million initial fees. Company-operated revenue was $9.690 billion and other revenue $647 million. Franchise margins were $13.930 billion versus $1.422 billion company-operated restaurant margins, demonstrating why shifting the mix toward franchises changes reported margins without necessarily increasing consumer demand. The company’s economic claim depends on healthy franchisee cash after food, labor, rent, franchise fees and reinvestment. A minimum-rent contract is not a guarantee that a distressed operator can pay. [issuer-annual]

Cash OCF of $10.551 billion and capex of $3.365 billion yielded historical native FCF of $7.186 billion. Capex rose from $2.775 billion in 2024 and $2.357 billion in 2023, so “franchise-heavy” is not capital-free. Annual 2026 plans called for $3.7–$3.9 billion capex and around 2,600 gross openings; those plans are distinct from achieved 2025 cash spending and must be read alongside the later NEXT roadmap. [issuer-annual]

**The September 23 NEXT strategy changes the near-term franchise economics underwriting.** A primary SEC-exhibit search observation sets approximately $5 billion partnering support through 2030 as part of $8.5 billion through 2036; 2027–2030 approximately $3 billion annual baseline capex plus $1.5–$2 billion cumulative capital partnering support, and low-to-mid-50% operating margin by 2030. Full strategy filing bodies were inaccessible; Reuters reporting independently observed rent relief/capital support, execution shortfalls, at least $1.2 million average US remodeling/upgrades and exclusion of remodel costs from company support. These are targets and reported requirements, not earned margin or committed support cash already paid. [strategy-primary-search; strategy-reuters]

Borrowings of $39.973 billion are a narrow debt measure, separate from $14.841 billion of current/noncurrent lease obligations. The annual report describes 97% fixed-rate debt, average rate around 4% and substantial foreign-currency exposure. That reduces immediate rate-reset risk but does not erase future refinancing or lease burdens. Year-end cash was just $774 million and book equity negative; repurchase-driven negative equity makes naive ROE analysis misleading. Franchise cash durability helps debt service, but leverage amplifies a traffic or franchisee-health disappointment. [issuer-annual]

## Moat

Brand recognition, locations, global procurement, reproducible operating processes and a franchise network reinforce each other. Long-duration site control is more durable than a single menu hit. Scale supports marketing and technology deployment, while franchisee local capital improves expansion economics. The defensibility is a system advantage, not an automatic consumer lock-in: diners can switch with negligible financial cost and franchise operators can resist burdensome programs. [issuer-annual; analysis]

A productive unit and cooperative franchisees are moat evidence; a high franchised accounting margin alone is not. Reuters reports a third of franchisees did not participate in the under-$3 menu and management acknowledged execution shortcomings. Remodeling mandates and rent relief indicate the system must reinvest to retain relevance. The moat weakens if customer value perception deteriorates, restaurant returns cannot fund required upgrades or new units cannibalize existing traffic. [strategy-reuters; analysis]

## Management

Capital priorities are reinvestment, dividends and repurchases. FY2025 repurchases were about $2 billion for 6.7 million shares; dividends plus repurchases returned approximately $7.1 billion. Those distributions nearly consume annual FCF before the newly detailed franchise support program. More leverage-funded shareholder returns cannot be assumed harmless because royalty revenue is recurring. [issuer-annual; analysis]

FY2025 net pre-tax charges were $229 million, reducing diluted EPS $0.25; adjusted EPS was $12.20 versus GAAP $11.95. Accelerating the Organization restructuring accounted for $226 million and continued costs were expected into 2027. The model uses GAAP rather than capitalizing every “one-off” addback; recurring transformation outlays and NEXT support are genuine claims on owner cash. Management’s 2030 margin/efficiency targets are evidence of intent, not demonstrated capital returns. Executive pay metrics and insider ownership are not independently audited here. ## Inversion

1. **Franchisee economics break before corporate royalties:** traffic weakness, labor/food inflation, discounts and at least $1.2 million upgrade burden erode local cash. Fee collection can look healthy temporarily while store reinvestment is deferred. [strategy-reuters]
2. **“Asset-light” becomes support-heavy:** rent relief and capital partnership depress cash while baseline capex and debt/lease service persist. Applying an old FCF yield to a new support plan overstates distributable cash.
3. **Value reputation erodes:** menu price offsets shrinking transactions until competitors take occasions; a promotion does not prove durable traffic recovery. Reuters quotes management expecting flat industry traffic while inflation is elevated. [strategy-reuters]
4. **Leverage and terminal multiple:** fixed coupons delay repricing but cannot protect a premium P/E or refinance forever. Lease/site commitments are omitted from the native narrow debt number, not from the real economic obligation.
5. **Food safety/brand or international disruption:** centralized scale allows a local incident or geopolitical backlash to travel globally; currency translation and franchisee operating conditions differ across markets. [issuer-annual]

Bear/base cases incorporate slower earnings and a lower/premium multiple rather than hiding execution failure in the 9.5% discount rate. Invalidate the constructive watchlist thesis if traffic and franchisee cooperation fail to improve, support costs become open-ended, or debt-funded distributions rise while unit returns decline. The retained primary strategy search excerpt is insufficient to declare the program’s contractual obligations fully understood.

## Industry

Quick-service restaurants compete on convenience, location, speed, price and food relevance, with a spending-constrained consumer and labor-intensive franchisees. McDonald’s scale can negotiate supply and advertise effectively, but cannot exempt operators from commodity, wage and rent inflation. Delivery/digital convenience adds occasions while creating platform fees and operating complexity. [issuer-annual; analysis]

NEXT responds to quality, hospitality, value and efficiency rather than simply pursuing more stores. Reuters reports experimentation with higher-protein options and portion flexibility; the investment thesis need not assume a specific GLP-1-driven demand forecast. New units contribute to sales targets but can lower returns if system expansion outruns incremental demand. The Treasury 10-year 5.24% yield is a real opportunity cost against a premium consumer franchise with high leverage. [strategy-reuters; treasury-20261009]

## Decision

**Watchlist, not an automatic defensive buy at $235.23.** The base price-only CAGR is 7.36%, below a 9.5% hurdle, with weighted discounted modeled value $200.58/share. A dividend adds to total return but is excluded from the native price-only model and competes with lease/debt service and franchise support. A separate dividend-inclusive cash-flow model would be needed to certify total-return adequacy.

Revisit on transaction-led comparable sales, franchisee upgrade participation and unit paybacks, detailed support cash timing, rent relief and net borrowing discipline. The durable brand/site system is attractive, but current pricing does not provide enough margin of safety for a program with meaningful upfront cash demands and incomplete primary current-strategy access.
