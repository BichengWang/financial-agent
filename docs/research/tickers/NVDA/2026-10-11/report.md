## Business

Cloud providers, model builders and enterprises pay NVIDIA for accelerated compute plus networking/software because time-to-usable performance matters; alternatives include AMD, custom accelerators and less compute-intensive models. The repeat purchase is a workload and platform upgrade, not a guaranteed subscription annuity.

## Industry

The value chain runs foundry/packaging and HBM → accelerator/CPU/network platform → cloud/operator → application customer. AI inference, longer-context models and scientific computing can sustain compute demand [issuer-latest], but custom silicon, model efficiency, energy and financing determine which vendor captures it. AMD's named rival platform and Arm CPU ecosystem are observed competitive routes, not guaranteed displacements. Numerical TAM is omitted: issuer superlatives and financing announcements are not audited serviceable demand. [INFERENCE] Durable institutional relevance is plausible; permanent75% chip gross margins are a separate and weaker thesis.

## Decision

**Watchlist.** Duan's business-quality lens is favorable for repeat platform usefulness; Buffett's durability lens is medium-high for ecosystem relevance but lower for current rents; stewardship is mixed due to expanding financing/investment exposure; Munger inversion emphasizes concentrated counterparties and commitments; Li Lu's productivity trend is strong without proving NVIDIA captures all gains. Price attractiveness is not established by abundant coverage.

Review triggers: gross-margin contraction despite healthy demand; receivables materially outgrowing shipments; commitments becoming uneconomic; customer migration to custom/AMD inference; new export charges; evidence of attractive cash returns for actual AI applications. Thesis break: ecosystem portability materially erodes pricing while suppliers and financiers retain hard claims. Further diligence should study deployment migration costs and end-customer utilization, not just another promotional benchmark. Research confidence medium-high on financial transcription, medium on normalized earnings, low on tail-event probabilities.
