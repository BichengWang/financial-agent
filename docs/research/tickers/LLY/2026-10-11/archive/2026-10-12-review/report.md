## Business

Patients use Lilly medicines repeatedly because diabetes, obesity and other chronic conditions persist; insurers, governments and self-paying patients fund those prescriptions through wholesalers/pharmacies, with rival medicines, lifestyle intervention and surgery as alternatives. The business is discovery plus regulated manufacturing and negotiated access—not a consumer subscription with assured lifetime retention.

## Moat

| Candidate advantage | Evidence | Counterevidence / falsifier |
|---|---|---|
| Protected drug IP | Tirzepatide compound US2036, major Europe2037, Japan2040 [lly-10k] | Patent validity challenges and alternative molecules; patents do not protect net prices or payer preference |
| Clinical/regulatory package | FDA OSA indication supported by randomized trials [fda-osa] | AHI improvement is not demonstrated cardiovascular mortality reduction; contraindications and tolerability limit adoption |
| Manufacturing know-how | Large capital spending and multiyear supply commitments [lly-10k] | Novo and other well-funded competitors can expand; plant delays/recalls convert capital advantage into stranded assets |
| Commercial access | Government agreements and new Medicare channels [lly-q2] | Lower net prices offset volume; reimbursement may favor competitors |

[INFERENCE] The moat has strengthened over2021-25 through tirzepatide's commercialization, but its ten-year durability depends on replacing today's lead before IP expires. Scale is useful only if yield, reliability and realized margins beat alternatives; no independently observed unit-cost series proves this. Falsifiers: net-price erosion outpaces volume, formulary losses, or oral/next-generation rivals displace high-margin patients.

Clinical specificity matters: FDA's December2024 approval used two double-blind randomized52-week studies totaling469 adults without type2 diabetes; one group on PAP, another unable/unwilling to use it. Primary endpoint was change in apnea-hypopnea index, with meaningful improvement versus placebo [fda-osa]. It is not a head-to-head proof against semaglutide and not an all-population outcomes study. Rat thyroid C-cell tumors and MTC/MEN2 contraindications are safety evidence, not a claim that human thyroid cancer causality is established. Historical FDA warnings are dated; this report is not a current prescribing guide.

## Inversion

| Failure mechanism | Timing / severity | Warning / response / model treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Net-price pressure exceeds access gains | Immediate, high margin sensitivity | Track rebates and volume separately; bear growth6%, exit22× |
| Manufacturing overbuild or quality interruption | Multiyear, potentially severe | Inventory/prepayment growth, launch delays, capex commitments; no invented plant utilization |
| Novo/oral/next-generation competition | Through model horizon, high | Formulary positioning, adherence and endpoints; base growth slows to18% versus recent surge |
| Safety/litigation/IP loss | Discrete tail, potentially thesis-breaking | Company product-liability/340B and Hatch-Waxman disclosures [lly-q2]; not captured by a smooth EPS path |
| Rich multiple de-rates | Immediate or terminal | Positive earnings growth can still yield negative stock return |

Strongest bear case: a great therapy creates a large market, but payers capture savings through price concessions while shareholders fund capacity and clinical replacement. Comparing to old patent-cliff franchises is informative, not proof: present tirzepatide protection is longer but molecule concentration is rising. Clinical catastrophe, injunction or fraud cannot be honestly assigned a precise probability from public excerpts. Those remain unmodeled tails, not hidden in the hurdle rate.

## Industry

Value chain: molecule discovery → trials/approval → API/fill-finish → wholesaler/pharmacy → prescriber/patient; reimbursement/payer control crosses the whole chain. Chronic metabolic disease demand is durable, but eligible patients are not paying users and prescriptions are not patient retention. No numerical TAM is used because budget capacity, adherence and contraindications make headline populations poor revenue forecasts.

Novo's injectables/oral semaglutide, Lilly's oral Foundayo and pipeline mechanisms make delivery convenience, tolerability, clinical outcomes and net price simultaneous axes of competition [lly-q2,fda-osa]. Weight-loss percentage is not equivalent to cardiovascular/renal/OSA outcomes. Government access creates unit growth but also discounts; PBM formularies, prior authorization and cost sharing can stop adoption even where approval exists [lly-10k]. Manufacturing cannot scale merely by announcing dollars; regulatory release and actual product yield matter. [INFERENCE] Long-run category growth likely persists, while profit distribution between producers, payers and patients is much less certain.

## Decision

**Watchlist: excellent execution does not establish price attractiveness.** Duan's business lens supports repeated patient need; Buffett's durability lens finds real IP/manufacturing protection but concentration; stewardship is cautiously positive with unverified incentives; Munger's inversion finds payer capture/overbuild and discrete safety tails; Li Lu's structural lens supports long-term metabolic treatment without guaranteeing Lilly's share.

Base price CAGR is below the11% hurdle and weighted present model value below the observed quote. The bull outcome is attractive only with sustained execution and premium valuation. This is not a validated price target or allocation instruction. Review primary quarterly net-price/volume bridge, inventory/capacity cash conversion, Foundayo royalties/profitability, comparative trials and formulary access. A thesis-breaking trigger is durable profit decline despite rising treatment volume, or material safety/IP impairment. Financial evidence confidence is medium-high; economic certainty remains materially lower.
