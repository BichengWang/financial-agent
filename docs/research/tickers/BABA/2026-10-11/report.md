## Grounding

BABA is the NYSE USD ADS of Cayman **Alibaba Group Holding Limited**; **one ADS represents eight ordinary shares**, also traded through Hong Kong 9988 (HKD) and 89988 (RMB) counters. It has **one share class and one vote per share**. The WVR designation instead arises from Alibaba Partnership's right, while the Partnership Condition holds, to nominate or occasionally appoint up to a simple board majority. Amending those rights requires at least 95% of shares voting. A generic ten-vote founder-share description would be wrong. The October 9, 2026 close was **USD111.37/ADS**, observed by independent market publishers. [issuer-annual; issuer-interim; quote-stockanalysis; quote-stockscan; quote-google]

No HKD-priced ordinary-share EPS is mixed with the USD ADS quote. [issuer-annual; issuer-interim]

The offshore structure includes directly owned subsidiaries and contractual VIEs for restricted PRC internet activities. ADS ownership does not supply direct equity rights in those VIEs. ## Business

Alibaba combines merchant monetization/commerce, logistics and cloud/AI. FY2026 revenue was **RMB1,023.670B/USD148.401B**. Operating income **RMB50.150B/USD7.270B fell 64%**, while adjusted EBITA fell 56%. Ordinary-attributable net income **RMB105.904B/USD15.353B** differs from consolidated net income RMB102.127B/USD14.805B. Non-GAAP net income RMB60.658B/USD8.794B fell 62%. Investment mark-to-market/disposal gains materially affect GAAP EPS; positive USD6.38 is not a clean recurring operating earning capacity. [issuer-annual]

Annual OCF was **USD11.049B**, versus **USD18.275B capital expenditures** (RMB126.063B). Issuer non-GAAP FCF **USD−6.757B** uses a different definition, with campus/land, intangibles and buyer-protection-deposit treatment differing from the native simple formula. Cash/equivalents **USD19.068B** is not USD75.504B cash/other-liquid-investments; restricted cash USD6.094B and short-term investments USD22.515B are separate. Estimated borrowing debt **USD37.692B** sums disclosed bank, senior, convertible and exchangeable balances, excluding operating leases. [issuer-annual]

**June 2026 changes the segment map.** E-commerce Group now combines domestic/international businesses and Freshippo; AI Cloud and Compute Services combines cloud with T-Head; AI Labs and Applications groups models, Qwen consumer and QwenWork; All Others remains. Comparisons require recast definitions. Quarter revenue was RMB268.953B (+9%); AI Cloud/Compute revenue RMB48.437B (+45%) and adjusted EBITA RMB5.628B (+133%). But AI Labs/Applications lost **RMB13.861B adjusted EBITA**; capital spending RMB67.678B/USD9.975B rose 75%. OCF USD3.382B did not cover investment: issuer FCF outflow was **USD6.584B**. [issuer-interim]

China customer-management revenue (CMR) fell **7% reported**, or grew **1% excluding development-program contra-revenue subsidies**. Weak transaction activity and subsidized monetization are more useful than an unqualified commerce-growth headline. International commerce revenue fell 1%, while AliExpress achieved management-reported operating profit. [issuer-interim]

## Moat

Merchant breadth, consumer purchase intent, integrated tools and approximately **64M 88VIP members at June 30** support commerce distribution. Cloud benefits from enterprise integration, domestic scale and a model/chip/compute stack. Accelerating AI-related cloud demand and improving cloud margin support actual monetization evidence, not just a capability story. [issuer-interim]

They do not prove group-level returns on AI capital. Competing clouds, open-model competition, compute pricing/export restrictions and inference costs can depress margins. Commerce merchants/users can multi-home; quick-commerce traffic may be purchased at poor economics. Newly separated AI-application losses make clear that profitable cloud does not automatically imply attractive AI-consumer economics. [issuer-annual; issuer-interim]

## Management

Joseph Tsai chairs the board; Eddie Wu is CEO and Toby Xu CFO in the latest release. Reorganization is substantive capital allocation: silicon/compute now sits with cloud, and model/application losses are more visible. Management reports improving quick-commerce unit economics and cloud operating leverage; these claims need to be tested against actual capital payments and sustained segment returns. [issuer-interim]

Partnership nomination rights constrain ordinary-shareholder influence even though every share has one vote. Their persistence through a change of control increases governance/realizability risk. June-quarter repurchases were only **USD162M for 13.4M ordinary shares**, amid multi-billion-dollar investment payments. Historical buyback scale is not a guaranteed current capital-return policy. [issuer-annual; issuer-interim]

## Inversion

The thesis fails if CMR cannot grow after subsidies, cloud growth requires indefinitely rising capital/inference subsidies, or AI-app distribution fails to become profitable paying demand. June-quarter ordinary profit fell 76%, non-GAAP net income 38%, and reported CMR 7%; cloud growth coexists with weaker group cash realization. Investment valuation gains can preserve headline EPS while operating returns weaken. [issuer-interim; issuer-annual]

Primary risks include PRC antitrust/data/cybersecurity rules, VIE enforceability, advanced-compute export restrictions, foreign platform/consumer obligations and partnership governance. The latest release records a **EUR550M European Commission Digital Services Act fine provision**—a sourced current event, not an invented forecast. Monitor cash burn, compute utilization and return, CMR after contra-revenue and earnings excluding investment gains. [issuer-interim; issuer-annual]

## Industry

Domestic commerce competes with PDD, JD and traffic platforms; instant delivery with established local-commerce networks; cloud/AI with domestic enterprise clouds and global alternatives where permitted. Gross direct-retail revenue, fee-based marketplace monetization and cloud-capacity revenue are not interchangeable peer measures. [issuer-annual; issuer-interim]

Qwen/T-Head/cloud integration may improve supply flexibility and enterprise offerings, but proprietary silicon is not proof future compute spending earns its cost. Latest cloud margin around 12% and strong AI demand must be weighed against AI-application losses and USD6.584B quarterly FCF outflow. [issuer-interim]

## Decision

**Wait for cash-flow evidence; no action supported.** Cloud growth and operating leverage are substantive, but AI-application losses, weak commerce monetization and large capital payments outweigh an attractive-sounding GAAP multiple. The package is **PROVISIONAL**, with estimated vendor current-cap/share basis and no defensible normalized earnings model. Reconsider after demonstrable post-investment cash returns under the new segment structure and an explicit willingness to accept governance/legal risks.
