## Industry

[INFERENCE] Site and electrical work are physical prerequisites for computing/industrial productivity, but contractors get paid for project delivery, not the eventual AI economic surplus. Transmission, generation, cooling and permitting delays can all postpone otherwise promising sites. Reallocation from low-bid transportation makes sense when high-margin demand is scarce, but increases exposure to the same mission-critical cycle.

Sterling participates alongside other engineering/site/specialty electrical contractors; customer procurement, scarce skilled labor and regional execution capabilities determine bargaining power. Secondary market enthusiasm is not a quantified independently supported TAM. Issuer’s organic growth and project-award evidence justify conditional upside, not permanent 50% EPS growth. Model rates are materially lower and recognize acquisitions, execution constraints and cash reinvestment [issuer-q226].

## Decision

**Watchlist, provisional: current execution credible, conservative price case unattractive.** Duan’s customer-value test is met; Buffett-style durability is qualified by project rather than annuity economics; stewardship looks strategically coherent but needs acquisition returns; Munger inversion demands collections and project estimates; Li Lu’s infrastructure tailwind does not grant every contractor perpetual pricing power.

Review triggers: organic cash conversion, signed/unsigned distinction, repeat-phase conversion, project estimate revisions, labor retention, CEC/Stone Ridge returns and more balanced customer concentration. Thesis breaks if cost-to-complete or uncollected claims absorb reported margin, or if a mission-critical slowdown leaves acquisition-funded fixed costs stranded. AI research confidence is moderate for actual disclosures and low for customer/project underwriting; investment certainty is lower than the precision of the scenario outputs. No validated allocation recommendation is made.
