## Business

### Why customers pay
Consumers and fleets buy Tesla vehicles for electric transport, integrated software and charging convenience; utilities and households buy storage for reliability and power economics; they can switch to rival EVs, hybrids, public transport or competing storage suppliers.
Repeat monetization comes from charging, repair, insurance and supervised-driving subscriptions, while new vehicles and storage installations remain capital-intensive product sales. [tsla-10q] [tsla-q226]
This is a proven manufacturing-and-services business with speculative additional business models, not yet a demonstrated high-margin autonomy platform.

| Revenue driver | FY2025 reported revenue | Customer economics and constraints |
|---|---:|---|
| Automotive revenue line | $69.526 billion | Vehicles, leasing and regulatory credits; pricing, mix, battery cost and factory utilization matter [tsla-fy25] |
| Energy generation/storage | $12.771 billion | Utility/commercial Megapack and residential Powerwall; cell supply, installations and warranty exposure matter [tsla-fy25] |
| Services/other revenue line | $12.530 billion | Charging, repair, insurance and associated activity; growing installed fleet supports recurrence [tsla-fy25] |
| Autonomous fleets/Optimus | No separately supported earnings basis | Product rollout or employee tests do not establish profitable commercial scale [tsla-q226] |

Formal reportable segments are automotive and energy; the automotive segment includes services/other, so revenue-line presentation is not a three-segment profit allocation. [tsla-10q]
Q2 2026 geographic revenue was **$13.208 billion US, $4.675 billion China and $10.353 billion other international**, making tariffs, currency, approvals and China manufacturing strategically material. [tsla-10q]
No customer-level concentration percentages or battery-supplier margin splits were established; broad consumer demand should not be confused with unconcentrated input risk.

| Candidate advantage | Evidence | Counterevidence/competitor response | Durability and falsifier |

| Brand/software | Supervised subscription base 1.48 million Q2 2026 [tsla-q226] | Includes upfront purchasers, not pure monthly recurring subscribers; rebranding and safety scrutiny [reuters-europe] | Conditional; falling paid attach/retention at comparable pricing would falsify |

| Data/autonomy platform | Large installed fleet and issuer rollout claims [tsla-q226] | Raw miles do not prove causal safety superiority, monetizable network effects or regulatory acceptance [nhtsa-ea] | Unproven; independent safety and profitable driverless operations required |

Auto pricing power narrowed over the observed period: issuer automotive margin excluding credits fell from **27.0% FY2021 to 15.4% FY2025**. [tsla-fy25]
Owners have software familiarity and charging convenience, but replacement vehicles are contestable; no sourced churn dataset proves a durable consumer lock-in.
A well-funded rival can replicate EV manufacturing and storage products; Tesla's system integration and execution speed may be harder to match than any individual feature.
Ten-year durability is stronger for integrated transport/energy service capability than for assuming one camera-based autonomy route wins every geography.
Value capture can move to customers through price cuts and to suppliers through scarce cells; Tesla does not necessarily keep all technological cost savings.

## Inversion

### Strong opposing thesis and falsifiable risks
The strongest bull objection is that present GAAP earnings deliberately expense the engineering of a valuable autonomy/robotics franchise; a conventional EPS model could miss a successful platform transition.
That objection is plausible, but independent proof must show permitted driverless operation, safety-adjusted operating costs, repeat paid demand and per-share earnings rather than only rollout geography. [tsla-q226] [nhtsa-ea]
The strongest bear case is that automotive cash funds increasingly capital-heavy speculative projects while competitive price cuts, compensation and liability prevent promised platform economics from reaching owners.

| Failure mechanism | Timing/severity | Early warning and falsifier | Valuation treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price war and weak utilization | Multi-year; severe margin compression | ASP falling without offsetting unit costs; auto ex-credit margin fails recovery [tsla-q226] | Bear negative EPS growth; base requires recovery |
| Storage quality/cell constraints | Near/mid-term; cash and margin loss | Warranty reserves/charges rise while deployments grow [tsla-q226] | Bull assumes cost/quality improvement; not free deployment growth |
| Autonomy safety/permission failure | Discrete and ongoing; potentially catastrophic | Visibility warnings, recall/remedy, permit restrictions [nhtsa-ea] [reuters-europe] | No option-value credit; catastrophic tail outside three cases |
| Capital misallocation/conflicts | Ongoing; permanent owner-value loss | Related-party investments and capex grow without cash returns [tsla-10q] | Per-share earnings hurdles; governance veto remains separate |
| Dilution outruns earnings | Multi-year; economic ownership erosion | Award expense/earned voting rights versus diluted EPS [tsla-10q] | Growth assumptions are per share, not aggregate profit |
| Successful rival technology | Multi-year; autonomy/brand displacement | Competitor safety, cost and customer wins at comparable geography | No assumed monopoly or permanent winner multiple |

NHTSA engineering analysis EA26002, opened **2026-03-18**, reports **nine crashes and one fatality** in its table and concerns about degraded-visibility detection/alerts; this is not a final defect verdict or an exposure-adjusted crash rate. [nhtsa-ea]
It also notes potential under-reporting from data/labeling limitations, so issuer aggregate driving miles cannot substitute for comparable safety validation. [nhtsa-ea]
European branding changed to Assisted Driving and an EU-wide vote moved from October to at least December 2026; approval friction is current, not merely hypothetical. [reuters-europe]
Analogy: earlier transport technologies created social value without all manufacturers retaining excess returns. Tesla differs through software/integration, but the analogy warns against equating adoption with owner profits.
Bias check: neither a famous CEO nor a spectacular share price is evidence of profitable robotaxi density. Conversely, dismissing all option value merely because current margins are low would be anchoring on mature-auto economics.

## Industry

Transport electrification, reliable electricity and automation address enduring needs; their social utility does not fix Tesla's share of the resulting cash flows.
Value chain: minerals/cells and power electronics feed vehicles/storage; Tesla integrates hardware, software, charging/service; customers and grid operators choose on cost, performance, financing and safety.
Consumer vehicle replacement remains discretionary and interest-rate sensitive; stationary storage benefits from grid integration but faces project permitting, interconnection, supply and warranty constraints.
Battery suppliers can capture shortages; utilities can bargain on large projects; rivals can package hybrid or EV products with alternative software and service networks.
China is both production strength and competitive pressure: Reuters reports **95,366 Shanghai-built September 2026 vehicles versus 90,812 a year earlier**, including exports, not just Chinese retail sales. [reuters-china]
Reuters reports selected China final-payment discounts of **7,000 yuan on Model Y and 5,000 yuan on Model 3 through October 2026**; currency amounts are source evidence, not converted into canonical USD financial facts. [reuters-china]
Shanghai growth cannot establish Tesla domestic share gains against BYD or other local rivals; no independently sourced competitor margin/share table was established here.
Autonomy substitutes include human-driven ride-hailing and competing sensor/operational approaches; regulation, insurance, remote support, cleaning, charging and utilization constrain the serviceable economics.
Optimus substitutes include labor and task-specific automation; external purchase orders, uptime, total labor savings and support costs were not established. Initial internal training builds are not commercial demand. [tsla-q226]
No numerical TAM is retained: counting vehicles, robotaxis and rides simultaneously would double count value, and humanoid addressability is not an observed revenue forecast.
Scenario growth below is explicitly conditional per-share earnings recovery, not a sourced market-growth estimate.

### Suitable conditional earnings benchmark, not fabricated option valuation
EPS basis is **reported FY2025 GAAP diluted $1.08/share**, not normalized non-GAAP or analyst forward EPS. It includes existing software revenue and investment expense, so it is not a clean standalone auto/energy carve-out. [tsla-fy25]
No forecast EPS fact is invented; no 2026 SpaceX gain is projected, and no robotaxi/Optimus revenue assumption is represented as an existing fact.
The model configures **five EPS compounding steps** from historical FY2025 earnings and assumes a **five-year holding/discount period**. Literal fiscal increments yield FY2030, not a verified FY2031 price forecast; treating $1.08 as current initial earning capacity is an unverified run-rate normalization, with no intervening earnings bridge or exact target date established.

The rate expresses the owner's desired compensation for cyclical capital-intensive equity exposure; it does not quantify an unmodeled catastrophic safety or governance outcome.

A successful AI transition could overturn this conclusion; assigning a convenient balancing option value today would conceal, rather than solve, the underwriting uncertainty.

## Decision

### Evidence-linked research judgment
**Avoid at $382.70 under demonstrated-earnings underwriting; watchlist for new operating evidence, not a definitive allocation recommendation.**
Business quality: integrated auto/energy/services has genuine products and cash generation; confidence medium-high in reported operations, lower in normalized future margins. [tsla-fy25] [tsla-q226]
Durability: charging, integration and storage execution matter, but pricing and competitive responses constrain excess returns; confidence medium. [tsla-q226] [reuters-china]
Stewardship: ambitious reinvestment and milestone retention coexist with substantial compensation, related-party exposure and key-person dependence; confidence mixed. [tsla-10q]
Industry opportunity: enduring transport/grid needs support demand, but safety, project economics and rival routes prevent assuming Tesla captures all productivity gains. [nhtsa-ea] [reuters-europe]
Price: the current quote is well outside the conditional operating-earnings present range; the model alone cannot value a successful new platform, so the research decision remains conditional.

### Review triggers and thesis breaks
- Q3 financial release, shown for **2026-10-21** by quote publishers: inspect ASP/mix, auto ex-credit margins, storage warranty cost, expense/SBC and cash capex, not just delivery headlines. [sa-quote] [wsz-quote]
- Require comparable independent safety data and final regulatory outcomes; investigate intervention/remote-support costs and liability before treating driverless expansion as scalable profit. [nhtsa-ea]
- Require fleet unit economics after insurance, maintenance, cleaning, charging and depreciation; paid miles and city counts alone cannot close this question. [tsla-q226]
- Require external Optimus customers, repeat orders and task productivity evidence; factory capacity and internal training are insufficient. [tsla-q226]
- Refresh actual shares/earned award rights and related-party investment governance; EPS growth must outpace economically relevant dilution. [tsla-10q]
- Thesis breaks on persistent price cuts without cost offsets, deteriorating warranty economics, unmanageable safety remedies or unacceptable conflict safeguards.
- Reconsider the bearish price conclusion if independently demonstrated new-business per-share earnings materially change the anchor, or a materially lower quote creates margin of safety without requiring option success.
If the market closed for five years, product demand, liquidity and service infrastructure could support business survival; they do not alone justify paying the current price.
Research confidence is materially higher than investment certainty for historical accounting, and materially lower for new-platform economic outcomes; no broker actions or orders are proposed.
