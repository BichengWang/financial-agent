# Moat

**Inputs:** Business model, customer behavior and supported economics from Business.

**Steps:**
1. Test pricing power (price versus retention/volume), switching costs (migration effort and actual churn), network effects (incremental participant benefit), scale (unit-cost evidence), and technology/IP (replication and expiry). A large company is not automatically protected.
2. For each candidate moat, give evidence, counterevidence, competitor response and falsifying indicator. Identify who captures the value: company, customer, supplier or employee.
3. Assess widening/narrowing over the observed five years and plausible persistence over ten years. Distinguish durable advantage from a temporary product lead or favorable cycle.

**Output:** `## Moat`, moat/evidence/counterevidence/durability table and confidence grounded in sources.

**Failure path:** unsupported advantages remain hypotheses; missing retention, share or pricing evidence lowers confidence, not automatically business quality.

**Question:** Could a well-funded competitor replicate this business, and what would destroy the advantage in ten years?
