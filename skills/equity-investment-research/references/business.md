# Business

**Inputs:** Grounding and its cited statement/segment evidence.

**Steps:**
1. State in one sentence who pays, for what, why they return, and what alternative they have: Duan Yongping's “right business” lens.
2. Map segments, geography and customers; distinguish subscriptions, repeat purchases, transaction revenue and one-time sales. Explain cost drivers, reinvestment needs and operating leverage.
3. Describe available five-year revenue, gross/operating margins, earnings and cash-flow trends, with periods and gaps. Cite source data; do not invent a continuous series or compute unsupported ratios mentally.
4. Explain economic versus accounting profit: dilution, capitalized costs, cyclic working capital, unusual items and cash conversion. Assess customer/supplier concentration and whether consolidated results obscure a weak segment.
5. When the plan selects `earnings-analysis`, load `skills/earnings-analysis/SKILL.md` after Grounding and retain its latest-results, guidance and thesis-delta analysis in the same ticker/date directory. Keep the core report's Business section distinct from the optional earnings report.

**Output:** `## Business`, business-model statement, revenue-driver table, evidenced quality/trend discussion and circle-of-competence boundary.

**Failure path:** unknown segment economics or unavailable trends => explicit provisional sub-conclusions; inability to explain why customers pay => thesis not established, not a fabricated moat.

**Question:** What makes this a good business in one sentence, and could it endure a five-year market closure?
